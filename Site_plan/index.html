Argentina Through Time

An educational journey through Argentine history, beginning with the declaration of independence in 1816 and continuing through the country's political, social, economic, and cultural development to 2026.

Site Plan

This planning section explains the purpose, audience, visual identity, typography, and responsive structure of the proposed history website.

Site Name

Argentina Through Time

The name was selected because it is short, memorable, and immediately communicates that the website explores Argentina across different historical periods.

Site Purpose

The purpose of the website is to provide an accessible educational overview of Argentina's history from independence in 1816 to the present day.

The site covers major political developments, constitutional changes, economic transformations, military periods, democratic transitions, social movements, cultural developments, and important national events.

Scenarios

Potential visitors may ask questions such as:

  • What happened when Argentina declared its independence in 1816?
  • How did Argentina develop from a collection of provinces into a modern republic?
  • What were the major military governments and democratic transitions of the 20th century?
  • What major events have shaped Argentina during the 21st century?

Color Scheme

The color palette is inspired by Argentina's national colors while maintaining good readability.

Navy Blue #12355B — headings, navigation, buttons
Sky Blue #5DA9E9 — accents, borders, timeline
Light Blue #EAF4FB — backgrounds and information cards
White #FFFFFF — primary page background and contrast

Typography

Georgia is used for headings. Its traditional serif style gives the history website an academic and historical appearance.

Arial is used for body text, navigation, labels, and supporting information. It provides clear readability on both desktop and mobile screens.

Target Audience

The primary audience includes students, teachers, travelers interested in Argentine history, and general readers who want a concise introduction to the country's historical development.

Responsive Wireframe

The wireframe focuses on structure rather than final visual decoration. The same content adapts from a two-column desktop layout to a single-column mobile layout.

Argentine History: 1816–2026

Argentina's modern national history is often traced through the independence process that began with the May Revolution of 1810 and reached a major milestone on July 9, 1816. On that date, representatives meeting in the Congress of Tucumán formally declared the independence of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata from Spanish rule.

Independence did not immediately create the centralized Argentine state that exists today. Much of the nineteenth century involved conflicts over political organization, the relationship between Buenos Aires and the provinces, federalism, economic development, and the boundaries of national institutions.

During the twentieth century, Argentina experienced periods of democratic government, military rule, economic transformation, social change, and major political movements. Since the restoration of constitutional democracy in 1983, the country has continued to develop through elected governments and significant economic and social challenges.

1816

Declaration of Independence

On July 9, 1816, the Congress of Tucumán declared the independence of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata from the Spanish monarchy and from other foreign domination.

The declaration represented a major constitutional and political milestone in the independence process that had begun with the May Revolution of 1810.

1820–1852

Provincial Autonomy and Political Conflict

After independence, the provinces struggled over how the new political system should be organized. Competing visions of centralism and federalism contributed to prolonged political conflict.

Buenos Aires became an especially important political and economic center, while provincial leaders defended their autonomy.

1829–1852

The Era of Juan Manuel de Rosas

Juan Manuel de Rosas became one of the dominant political figures in Buenos Aires and the Argentine Confederation during the first half of the nineteenth century.

His period of influence was characterized by strong political authority, conflict between federal and unitary factions, and continued disagreement over the organization of the country.

1853–1862

Constitution and National Unification

The Constitution of 1853 established a federal framework for the Argentine nation. Buenos Aires initially remained outside the Confederation but later rejoined the national political system.

By 1862, national political institutions had been consolidated around a unified Argentine state.

1860s–1880s

Nation Building and Economic Expansion

During the second half of the nineteenth century, the Argentine state expanded its administrative institutions, transportation networks, and economic connections with international markets.

Agricultural exports, railways, immigration, and urban growth became important features of national development.

1880–1916

The Conservative Order

Buenos Aires became the federal capital in 1880. Argentina experienced rapid economic growth and large-scale immigration during this period.

Political participation remained limited, while a small political elite exercised substantial influence over national government.

1916

Expansion of Electoral Democracy

Electoral reforms associated with the Sáenz Peña Law helped establish secret and compulsory male voting and changed the country's political system.

Hipólito Yrigoyen of the Radical Civic Union became president in 1916, marking a major transition in national electoral politics.

1930–1943

The Infamous Decade

A military coup overthrew President Yrigoyen in 1930. The following decade was marked by political instability, electoral manipulation, and changing relationships between civilian governments and the military.

1943–1955

The Rise of Peronism

Juan Domingo Perón became a central figure in Argentine politics. His governments promoted labor organization, social welfare policies, industrialization, and a larger role for the state in the economy.

Eva Perón also became an important political and social figure, particularly through her work with workers and women's political participation.

1955–1976

Political Instability

After Perón was overthrown in 1955, Argentina experienced alternating civilian and military governments. Peronism was restricted for significant periods, while political conflict remained intense.

Economic problems, social conflict, political violence, and military intervention shaped the period.

1976–1983

Military Dictatorship

A military coup in 1976 established a dictatorship known as the National Reorganization Process.

The period included systematic state repression, kidnappings, torture, and disappearances. Thousands of people were killed or disappeared during the period of state terrorism.

1982

Falklands/Malvinas War

Argentina and the United Kingdom fought a war over the Falkland Islands, known in Argentina as the Islas Malvinas.

Argentina surrendered in June 1982. The war had major political consequences for the military government and contributed to the transition toward democracy.

1983

Return to Democracy

Democratic government was restored in 1983. Raúl Alfonsín became president following national elections.

The new democratic period included efforts to investigate human rights abuses committed during the dictatorship and to strengthen constitutional institutions.

1989–1999

Economic Reform and the 1990s

Carlos Menem's governments introduced major economic reforms, including privatization, deregulation, and the Convertibility system linking the peso to the U.S. dollar.

The period brought economic stabilization in its early years but also increased public debt, unemployment, and economic vulnerabilities.

2001–2002

Economic and Political Crisis

Argentina experienced a severe economic and political crisis. The government defaulted on its public debt, the banking system faced restrictions, and widespread protests occurred.

President Fernando de la Rúa resigned in December 2001. Several presidents followed in a short period before political stability returned.

2003–2015

The Kirchner Era

Néstor Kirchner became president in 2003, followed by Cristina Fernández de Kirchner, who served as president from 2007 to 2015.

The governments emphasized social programs, human-rights policies, domestic consumption, and a stronger role for the state in economic policy.

2015–2019

The Macri Presidency

Mauricio Macri became president in 2015. His administration sought to change economic policy, attract investment, reduce some government controls, and restore access to international financial markets.

Argentina experienced renewed economic difficulties, including inflation, currency depreciation, and increased reliance on international financing.

2019–2023

The Alberto Fernández Presidency

Alberto Fernández became president in 2019. His government faced continuing inflation and economic difficulties, as well as the COVID-19 pandemic beginning in 2020.

The pandemic affected public health, education, employment, economic activity, and daily life throughout the country.

2023–2026

The Current Period

Javier Milei became president in December 2023. His administration introduced a program focused on reducing inflation, limiting government spending, deregulating parts of the economy, and changing the role of the state.

As of 2026, Argentina continues to debate the economic and social effects of these policies, while the country remains a constitutional democratic republic.

Major Historical Periods

Independence

1810–1820: Revolution, independence, military campaigns, and the first attempts to organize the new nation.

Nation Building

1820–1880: Provincial conflict, federalism, constitutional development, and national unification.

Export Economy

1880–1916: Immigration, agriculture, railways, urbanization, and rapid economic growth.

Mass Politics

1916–1955: Expanded electoral participation, Radical governments, military intervention, and the rise of Peronism.

Instability

1955–1983: Political conflict, military governments, social unrest, state repression, and the 1982 Malvinas/Falklands War.

Democratic Era

1983–2026: Democratic institutions, economic reforms, financial crises, social change, and continuing debates about Argentina's economic and political direction.

Understanding Argentina Today

Modern Argentina is the result of more than two centuries of political, economic, cultural, and social change. Understanding the country today requires looking at the relationship between its independence, federal institutions, economic development, immigration, labor movements, military interventions, democratic governments, and recurring economic crises.

History also helps explain why events such as the Constitution of 1853, the expansion of voting, Peronism, the military dictatorship, the return to democracy in 1983, the 2001 economic crisis, and the economic changes of the 2020s remain important parts of Argentina's public memory.
